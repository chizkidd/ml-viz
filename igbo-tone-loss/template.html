Igbo Tone-Loss Explorer

Igbo is a tonal language where pitch distinguishes words and careful orthography marks tone (grave, acute) alongside dot-below vowels (ị, ọ, ụ). This explorer inspects 21 recordings from one speaker, run through facebook/omniASR-CTC-1B, aligned with the audited ANALYSIS_SPEC.md contract. Data: measured from omniASR-igbo-blindspots; alignments, exact resample distributions, and charts are computed live.

Primary endpoint (E2, conditional on single-annotator unreviewed references): Across the 4 Igbo tonal pilot clips (06–09), references carry 32 tone-marked letters and the output carries 3 (all in clip 09, whose reference has none). Under base-letter-aware alignment, 0 of 32 reference tone marks are reproduced: 28 missing on a matching base letter, 4 on a substituted base letter, 0 base-deleted. Language-based analysis_groups are the default view; mixed-language task categories are preserved as a historical toggle.

Why no error bars? Under docs/ANALYSIS_SPEC.md §6.4, an interval requires both a language-based reporting unit and at least 10 informative clips (clips carrying ≥1 expected mark). No group reaches 10 informative clips (largest is igbo_other at 7 for all/dot, 1 for tone; igbo_tonal_pilot has 3), and overall / historical categories aggregate languages and export chains. All bootstrap CIs and paired contrasts are therefore withdrawn; bars show counts + LOO sensitivity ranges only.

Base-letter-aware mark alignment (Primary endpoint)

8-class alignment
Reference and output are aligned on base letters first (Needleman-Wunsch, match +2, mismatch −1, gap −1). In the repaired base-letter-aware rule, a reference mark is only judged when its base letter matches; marks on mis-heard or deleted letters are broken out separately as base_substituted and base_deleted instead of being credited or conflated.

Mark confusion matrix (matching base letters)

Rows: reference mark on a letter. Columns: model mark on the aligned letter (when base letter matches under the repaired rule).

Alignment ambiguity (n_optimal_alignments)

Count of equally scoring optimal alignments under the DP scoring rule. 1 means unique (true for all 4 Igbo tonal pilot clips 06–09); >1 means tie-breaks can affect individual letter pairings (up to 480 for French clip 19).

What this can and cannot show

What it supports (up to E2)

  • Conditional on the author's references, the output text on the 4 Igbo tonal clips carries 3 tone marks against 32 in the references, and 0 of 32 are reproduced under base-letter-aware alignment (28 missing on a matching base letter, 4 on substituted base letters; optimal alignment unique for all 4 clips).
  • Separating strict CER (0.416 on igbo_tonal_pilot) from tone_stripped (0.197) and base_letters (0.121) shows that transcription errors and mark loss occur together.
  • Exact DP resampling shows why small-n clip groups produce coarse discrete supports (e.g. 169 distinct rates across 6 clips) and cannot justify population error bars.

What it does not show

  • General Igbo ASR performance: one speaker (Afikpo Igbo), 21 clips, one session/device, one model (omnilingual-asr 0.1.0 also ignores lang for CTC models).
  • Acoustic tone perception or causal mechanism: clip 09 is flatter than clip 08 (F0 SD 1.59 vs 2.71 st, one production each), not proven monotone, and added dot-below letters there may be valid orthography.
  • Reference correctness, glosses, or minimal-pair contrasts: single annotator, no independent fluent-Igbo review (U-1); clip 06's exploratory F0 check did not show four distinct contours.
  • Clean between-category comparisons: clips 01–05 use a 16 kHz / 22 kbps gated export chain (36–39% digital silence) while clips 06–21 are 48 kHz / 65 kbps.
